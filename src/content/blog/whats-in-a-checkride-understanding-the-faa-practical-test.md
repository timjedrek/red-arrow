For every aspiring pilot training at Red Arrow Flight Academy in Santa Teresa, NM, the FAA checkride is the final step between student pilot and certified aviator. It is the moment where all your training, study, and flight hours come together in a single day to prove you are ready to fly on your own. Whether you are pursuing your Private Pilot Certificate or planning to go further into a professional flying career, understanding what the checkride involves can help you feel more confident and prepared.

## What Exactly Is a Checkride?

The FAA Practical Test, commonly known as the checkride, is a formal evaluation that tests both your aeronautical knowledge and flight skills. It is administered by a Designated Pilot Examiner, or DPE, who is approved by the FAA to assess pilot applicants. While it might feel intimidating, the checkride is not designed to trick you. It is meant to confirm that you can safely operate an aircraft within the parameters of the Federal Aviation Regulations and the Airman Certification Standards.

At Red Arrow, we make sure our students understand the structure of the checkride and what to expect well before they are scheduled to take it.

## Two Parts: Oral and Flight Test

The checkride has two main components: an oral exam and a flight test. It usually begins on the ground, with the DPE conducting a detailed discussion of your flight planning and decision-making. This portion can take between 1.5 to 3 hours depending on the examiner and your preparedness.

During the oral portion, you might be asked to:

- Explain the details of a cross-country flight you have planned
- Discuss airspace classifications and VFR weather minimums
- Interpret weather reports and forecasts
- Describe aircraft systems and performance
- Answer questions about regulations and safety procedures

Your instructor at Red Arrow will work closely with you during ground training to ensure you are confident in each of these areas.

After successfully completing the oral exam, the flight portion follows. You will fly with the examiner and demonstrate key maneuvers such as:

- Normal and crosswind takeoffs and landings
- Slow flight and stalls
- Steep turns and ground reference maneuvers
- Navigation and diversion procedures
- Emergency procedures and system failures
- Use of cockpit checklists and situational awareness

The DPE is evaluating your decision-making, ability to maintain control of the aircraft, and whether you can perform safely within the required standards.
